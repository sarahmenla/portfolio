A study in human–computer interaction

Three web-based interfaces for children with learning differences, older adults accessing healthcare, and women with visual impairments shopping for clothing. The same HCI theories run through all three, but they are applied differently for each audience — not as one set of rules for everyone.

Approach

One process, three very different audiences

Research before pixels

Each product starts from a persona grounded in published research. The learning platform also drew on a short survey.

Theory, not taste

Hick’s and Fitts’s Laws, Nielsen’s heuristics, Gestalt principles, cognitive load theory and WCAG 2.1 justify each decision.

Designed for the edges

Fewer, calmer choices for children; larger targets and plain language for older adults; everything in words for women with visual impairments.

Honest evaluation

The learning platform’s tasks were tested with three adult proxies, after a short survey of four children. DentaCare had a heuristic review, and the e-commerce platform a heuristic review and a WCAG 2.1 audit.
